\begin{abstract}
For a given dataset of observations maximize the likelihood of the causal graph that generates the reconstruction data.
Estimate the likelihood of the DAG in the linear causal model.
Use Markov equivalence classes of DAGs.
\end{abstract}

There given~$m$ observations $\mathbf{x}\in\mathbb{R}^n$ %$\bD = \{\mathbf{x}_i\in\mathbb{R}^n\}$
of a random variable~$X$.
The structural causal model is
\begin{equation}
    \hat{\bx} = \bW\mathbf{x} + \boldsymbol{\varepsilon}, \qquad \boldsymbol{\varepsilon} \sim \mathcal{N}(\mathbf{b}, \bSig),
    \label{eq:lin_scm}
\end{equation}
$\bSig = \mathrm{diag}(\sigma_1^2, \dots, \sigma_n^2)$ is the diagonal noise covariance.
Denote by~$f$ the model~\eqref{eq:lin_scm} and its parameters $\bw = (\bW, \mathbf{b})$. %text(vec)
The directed acyclic graph~$G$ defines the structure of the model~$f$, so that for
\[
    \fG \defeq f(\mathbf{x}; \bw, G): \bx \mapsto \hat{\bx}
\]
a model parameter $w_{j} = 0$ if the corresponded element of adjacency matrix~$\bG$ is zero.

The problem is to estimate the most probable DAG structure $p(G\mid X)$ given
%the data $\bD$
the observed variable $X$ and the model $f$:
\begin{equation}
    p(G \mid X, \fG) = p(X \mid \fG) p(G) \to \max_{G},
    \label{eq:bayes_score}
\end{equation}
where $p(G)$ is a prior over DAGs and $p(X \mid \fG)$ is the likelihood of the data given the DAG structure.

The term $p(X \mid \fG)$ is the marginal likelihood of the data given the DAG structure, obtained by integrating out the parameters $\bw$:
\begin{equation}
    p(X \mid \fG) = \int p(X \mid \bw, \fG) p(\bw \mid \fG) d\bw
    \label{eq:marginal_likelihood}
\end{equation}

where $p(\bw \mid \fG)$ is the prior over the parameters given the DAG structure.

Assume the distribution of the model parameters $p(\bw \mid \fG) = \cN(\bmu, \bA)$ and
with the assumption from~\eqref{eq:lin_scm} rewrite the integrated part from likelihood term~\eqref{eq:marginal_likelihood} as:
\begin{equation}
    p(\bw \mid \bx, \fG) = \frac{1}{2} \sum_i (\bx_i - \hat{\bx}_i)\T \bSig^{-1} (\bx_i - \hat{\bx}_i)
    + \frac{1}{2} (\bw - \bmu)\T \bA^{-1} (\bw - \bmu).
    \label{eq:likelihood_prior}
\end{equation}
The parameters of the prior~$\bmu, \bA$ and covariance matrix~$\bSig$ is estimated during the optimization algorithm~\ref{alg:one}.

Assume several independent hypotheses of the graph~$G$ distribution over the set of DAGs:
\begin{enumerate}[1)]
    \item $p(G) = \text{const}$, the uniform prior,
    \item $p(G) \propto e^{-\gamma |G|}$, a sparsity-inducing prior,
    \item $p(G) \propto e^{-\gamma h(G)}$, where $h(G)$ is a smooth acyclicity constraint,% (NOTEARS),
    \item $p(G)$ is a Dirichlet distribution
    \[ \mathrm{Dir}(\mathbf{y} \mid \boldsymbol{\pi}) = \frac{\Gamma \left(\sum_{i=1}^k \pi_i\right)}
    {\prod_{i=1}^k \Gamma(\pi_i)}\prod_{i=1}^k y_i^{\pi_i - 1},
    \]
    \item $p(G) \propto \text{GS}(\boldsymbol{\pi},\tau)$, the concrete (relaxed) Gumbel-Softmax distribution
    \[
    p(\mathbf{y} \mid \boldsymbol{\pi}, \tau) = \Gamma(k),\tau^{k-1}
    \left( \sum_{i=1}^{k} \frac{\pi_i}{y_i^{\tau}} \right)^{-k}\prod_{i=1}^{k} \frac{\pi_i}{y_i^{\tau+1}}.
    \]
\end{enumerate}
The last two distributions have the parameters $\boldsymbol{\pi} = (\pi_1, \dots, \pi_i > 0, \dots, \pi_k)$, are the unnormalized log-probabilities of edges of the simplex

The Gamma function $\Gamma(k)$ has $k$ categories, edges of the graph $G$.

Solving for  $\mathbf{x}$:
$$\mathbf{x} = (I - W)^{-1}\boldsymbol{\varepsilon} \implies \mathbf{x} \sim \mathcal{N}!\left(\mathbf{0},; (I-W)^{-T}\Sigma(I-W)^{-1}\right)$$

the log-likelihood factorises node-by-node thanks to the DAG structure:
$$\log p(X \mid W, \Sigma) = \sum_{i=1}^{n} \sum_{k=1}^{m} \log \mathcal{N}!\left(x_i^{(k)};; W_{i,\mathrm{pa}(i)}, \mathbf{x}_{\mathrm{pa}(i)}^{(k)},; \sigma_i^2\right)$$
where $\mathrm{pa}(i)$ denotes the parents of node $i$ in the DAG.

Bayesian score
To marginalise over the parameters $(W, \Sigma)$, we place standard Gaussian priors on the edge weights and inverse-Gamma priors on the noise variances (the conjugate pair):
$$W_{ij} \sim \mathcal{N}(0, \lambda^{-1}), \quad \sigma_i^2 \sim \text{Inv-Gamma}(\alpha_0, \beta_0)$$
Because the DAG factorises, the Bayesian score also factorises:
$$\log p(X \mid \mathcal{G}) = \sum_{i=1}^{n} \log p!\left(X_i \mid X_{\mathrm{pa}(i)}\right)$$
Each local score $\log p(X_i \mid X_{\mathrm{pa}(i)})$ is obtained by closed-form marginalisation, yielding a Bayesian Gaussian equivalent (BGe) score. Let $k_i = |\mathrm{pa}(i)|$ be the number of parents of node $i$. Then:
\[
    \log p(X_i \mid X_{\mathrm{pa}(i)}) = \frac{1}{2}\log\frac{\lambda^{k_i}}{\lambda^{k_i+1}} + \log\frac{\Gamma(\alpha_0 + m/2)}{\Gamma(\alpha_0)} - \frac{m}{2}\log(2\pi) + \alpha_0\log\beta_0 - \left(\alpha_0 + \frac{m}{2}\right)\log\hat{\beta}_i
\]
where the posterior shape parameter is:
$$\hat{\beta}i = \beta_0 + \frac{1}{2}\left(\mathbf{x}i^\top \mathbf{x}i - \mathbf{x}i^\top X{\mathrm{pa}(i)}\left(X{\mathrm{pa}(i)}^\top X{\mathrm{pa}(i)} + \lambda I\right)^{-1} X{\mathrm{pa}(i)}^\top \mathbf{x}_i\right)$$
This term captures the residual sum of squares after regressing $x_i$ on its parents, regularised by the prior precision $\lambda$.

Key intuitions
The prior precision $\lambda$ acts as ridge regularisation in the local regression. As $\lambda \to 0$ (flat prior), the Bayesian score approaches the MLE log-likelihood. Larger $\lambda$ penalises complex parent sets more heavily.
The posterior $\hat{\beta}_i$ is the updated residual scale after seeing the data. If $x_i$ is well-explained by its parents, $\hat{\beta}_i$ stays close to $\beta_0$, yielding a high score. Poor-fitting parent sets inflate $\hat{\beta}_i$, reducing the score.
Factorisation means the total Bayesian score is just a sum of $n$ independent local scores — one per node — each computable in $O(k_i^3)$ time (matrix inversion over the parent set). This makes the score modular and efficient for structure search algorithms.

Great choice. The key challenge is that optimising over DAGs is combinatorially hard — the DAG constraint (acyclicity) is discrete. The NOTEARS framework (Zheng et al., 2018) resolves this by encoding acyclicity as a smooth algebraic constraint, enabling continuous optimisation.

NOTEARS formulation
The acyclicity constraint exploits the following result: a matrix $W$ is a DAG if and only if:
$$h(W) = \mathrm{tr}\left(e^{W \circ W}\right) - n = 0$$
where $\circ$ denotes elementwise squaring and the matrix exponential counts directed cycles of all lengths — it equals zero iff no cycles exist.

Augmented Lagrangian problem
We maximise the sparse Bayesian score subject to $h(W) = 0$, reformulated as a minimisation via the augmented Lagrangian:
$$\min_{W}; \mathcal{L}(W) = \underbrace{-\sum_{i=1}^{n}\log p(X_i \mid X_{\mathrm{pa}(i)}; W)}{\text{negative BGe score}} + \underbrace{\gamma |W|1}{\text{sparsity}} + \underbrace{\alpha, h(W) + \frac{\rho}{2} h(W)^2}{\text{acyclicity penalty}}$$
where $\gamma$ controls sparsity, $\alpha$ is the Lagrange multiplier, and $\rho$ is the penalty coefficient, both updated across outer iterations.

Gradient of the acyclicity constraint
The gradient of $h(W)$ with respect to $W$ is:
$$\nabla_W h(W) = 2W \circ e^{W \circ W} \qquad \text{(elementwise)}$$
which is cheap to compute and differentiable everywhere — enabling standard gradient-based optimisers (L-BFGS, Adam).

Full algorithm---
Update equations
At each outer iteration $t$, after the inner minimisation converges:
$$\alpha^{(t+1)} = \alpha^{(t)} + \rho^{(t)} \cdot h(W^{(t)})$$ $$\rho^{(t+1)} = \mu \cdot \rho^{(t)}, \qquad \mu > 1$$
The Lagrange multiplier $\alpha$ accumulates the a
cyclicity violation, while $\rho$ grows geometrically to force $h(W) \to 0$.

Complete objective gradient
The full gradient passed to L-BFGS at each inner step is:
$$\nabla_W \mathcal{L} = \underbrace{-\nabla_W \sum_i \log p(X_i \mid X_{\mathrm{pa}(i)}; W)}{\text{negative BGe gradient}} + \underbrace{\gamma \cdot \mathrm{sign}(W)}{\ell_1\text{ sparsity}} + \underbrace{\left(\alpha + \rho, h(W)\right) \cdot 2W \circ e^{W \circ W}}_{\text{acyclicity}}$$
The BGe gradient w.r.t. $W_i$ (the $i$-th row of $W$, corresponding to the parents of $x_i$) is:
$$\nabla_{W_i} \log p(X_i \mid X_{\mathrm{pa}(i)}) = \frac{\alpha_0 + m/2}{\hat{\beta}i} \cdot \left(X{\mathrm{pa}(i)}^\top X_{\mathrm{pa}(i)} + \lambda I\right)^{-1} X_{\mathrm{pa}(i)}^\top \mathbf{x}_i$$

Complexity and practical notes
The dominant cost per gradient evaluation is computing $e^{W \circ W}$, which costs $O(n^3)$ via eigendecomposition. For large $n$, this can be approximated by truncating the matrix exponential series:
$$e^{W \circ W} \approx \sum_{k=0}^{K} \frac{(W \circ W)^k}{k!}$$
with $K = n$ terms sufficient to detect all cycles.
